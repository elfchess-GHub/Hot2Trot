Enclosure and Primitive Accumulation

1500s-1700s | Black road

Land, law, enclosure, debt, and forced market dependence help create the conditions for wage labor and capitalist property relations.

[source lead checked]

What Happened?

Enclosure means land that had been used through common or customary rights was fenced, legally enclosed, or converted into more exclusive private control. In England, this happened over a long period through local practice, landlord power, market pressure, and parliamentary enclosure.

Why This Stop Matters

This stop matters because capitalism is not only factories and inventions. Before factory wage labor becomes normal, many people have to lose older ways of living from land, commons, village rights, or subsistence. Enclosure helps show how property law can create freedom for owners and dependency for workers at the same time.

Key Terms

CommonsEnclosurePrivate propertyWage laborLand dispossessionPrimitive accumulation

What To Be Careful About

Do not reduce enclosure to one simple villain story. It involved law, agriculture, class power, market incentives, productivity arguments, and real displacement. The fair question is what kind of property system was being built, who gained power, and who lost independence.
