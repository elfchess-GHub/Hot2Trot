Civil Rights, Housing, and Racial Capitalism

1950s-1970s | Black road

Segregation, redlining, labor exclusion, policing, schools, housing, and property access shape who gets wealth and citizenship in American capitalism.

[source lead checked]

What Happened?

The civil rights era challenged legal segregation and racial exclusion, while housing struggles exposed redlining, discrimination, unequal credit access, and unequal property opportunity. These fights connected citizenship, schools, labor, policing, housing, voting, and wealth.

Why This Stop Matters

This stop matters because capitalism in America is not only markets and entrepreneurs. Property access, racial law, labor exclusion, public policy, and housing finance shaped who could build wealth and who was blocked from it.

Key Terms

Civil rightsSegregationRedliningHousing discriminationProperty accessRacial capitalismCitizenship

What To Be Careful About

Do not collapse all racial injustice into capitalism alone, and do not pretend capitalism sits outside racial law and property policy. The point is the connection: markets, state power, race, housing, and wealth worked together.
