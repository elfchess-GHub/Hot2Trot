Fall of the Soviet Union

1991 | Black road

The collapse reshapes arguments about planning, party-state rule, capitalism, democracy, and what communism meant in practice.

[needs verification]

What Happened?

The Soviet Union dissolved in 1991 after years of economic stagnation, political crisis, reform attempts, nationalist movements, institutional breakdown, and loss of legitimacy. The collapse ended one major party-state model that had claimed socialism and communism.

Why This Stop Matters

This stop matters because it reshaped the global argument. Some treated the collapse as proof that all socialism failed; others argued it proved the danger of authoritarian party-state rule, bureaucracy, and undemocratic planning. The page has to keep those claims separate.

What To Be Careful About

Do not use the Soviet collapse as a shortcut that settles every question about socialism, public goods, democracy, labor, or markets. It is evidence about a specific historical state and system of rule.
