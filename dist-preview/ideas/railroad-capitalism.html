Railroad Capitalism

Plain definition: Railroad capitalism is private railroad expansion built through public land grants, federal loans, finance, dangerous labor, racial hierarchy, market expansion, and western settlement.

Railroads were technology, but they were also land policy, labor policy, corporate power, and market-making infrastructure.

Why It Matters Here

Railroads moved crops, cattle, minerals, people, mail, armies, clocks, prices, and markets across the continent. They also connected private corporations to public land, public subsidies, immigrant labor, Native dispossession, and dangerous working conditions.

That makes railroads a clean example of a mixed mechanism: private accumulation did not float above the state. It often rode on state-granted land, law, finance, and force.

What To Be Careful About

Do say: railroads expanded markets and helped industrial growth.
Also say: railroad expansion depended on land grants, public support, exploited labor, and settlement across contested land.
Do not fake: worker death totals, land-transfer totals, or Indigenous death counts without pinned source passages.
