Capitalism

Plain definition: Capitalism is an economic system organized around private ownership of capital, markets, wage labor, investment, and profit.

For this project, capitalism cannot be taught only as markets and innovation. The American road also needs slavery, Native dispossession, land markets, railroads, finance, race law, and state force in the same frame.

Why It Matters Here

A truth-first study guide cannot count Stalin's failures and then soften capitalism's record. The careful version is not "capitalism alone did everything." The careful version is: capitalist accumulation often worked through state power, racial law, forced labor, land seizure, railroad expansion, and private profit.

Core Features

For this guide, the useful first features are private ownership of productive assets, markets, wage labor, capital accumulation, investment, competition, profit motive, and legal protection of property and contract.

What Counts as Capitalism?

A society can have markets without being purely capitalist, and capitalism can rely heavily on the state. The question is not whether government appears. The question is whether production, property, labor, investment, and profit are organized around capital accumulation and private control.

Attribution Rule

Do not fake exact death totals. Build source-pinned claims: who died, where, under what policy, through what economic mechanism, and which source supports the count.

What To Be Careful About

Do not use capitalism as a magic explanation for every harm, and do not let capitalism escape when state power protects private accumulation. Follow the mechanism: land, law, labor, race, markets, debt, police, courts, war, trade, or ownership.

Mini-Check

Why is state power still relevant when studying capitalism?

Because capitalist property, contracts, markets, land claims, corporations, and labor systems are often built, protected, expanded, or disciplined through law and state institutions.
