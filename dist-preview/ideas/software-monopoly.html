Software Monopoly

Plain definition: Software monopoly means a firm gains durable power over a digital market through operating systems, standards, licensing, defaults, bundling, network effects, or lock-in.

In the Gates path, monopoly is not only owning factories. It is controlling the platform where other software and users have to live.

Why It Matters Here

Software monopoly matters because modern capitalism can concentrate power through code, interfaces, contracts, and defaults, not only through land or machines. The lesson is that control over a platform can shape the choices available to customers, developers, competitors, and whole industries.
