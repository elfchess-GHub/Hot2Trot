Labor

Plain definition: Labor means human work inside an economy: time, skill, risk, discipline, bargaining power, and social value.

This page connects the Industrial Revolution, Engels, Marx, Debs, unions, and modern public policy.

Why It Matters Here

Labor is where big economic words touch daily life. Capitalism, socialism, unions, welfare states, public spending, and class politics all become concrete when you ask who works, who owns, who controls the pace, who takes the risk, and who receives the reward.

Careful Version

Work is not just a cost: it is time, body, skill, danger, dignity, and dependency.
Labor politics is not automatically socialism: unions and labor parties can be reformist, radical, conservative, democratic, exclusionary, or mixed.
Study question: what power does a worker have alone, and what changes when workers organize together?
