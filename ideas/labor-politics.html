Labor Politics

Plain definition: Labor politics means workers using unions, parties, strikes, campaigns, and public speech to change economic and political power.

In the Debs path, labor politics connects shop-floor conflict to elections and public argument.

Why It Matters for Debs

Labor politics matters because it shows democracy is not only voting. It is also the struggle over whether ordinary workers can organize power at work, in courts, in parties, in public speech, and in the street.

Where It Gets Tricky

Labor politics can be reformist or radical, narrow or broad, democratic or machine-like. The point is not to romanticize labor automatically. The point is to study how people without large property try to build leverage against people and institutions that do have it.
