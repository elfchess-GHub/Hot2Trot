Totalitarianism

Plain definition: Totalitarianism is a political concept for regimes that seek unusually broad control over public and private life through ideology, party power, surveillance, censorship, repression, and mass organization.

This word needs care because it is both a political-science concept and a Cold War argument word. It can clarify patterns of control, but it can also flatten history if used like a slogan.

Why It Matters Here

Totalitarianism matters because the page is studying the danger that a movement for order, revolution, nation, race, class, or security can try to occupy every part of public life. It belongs near Stalin, fascism, propaganda, Orwell, and Cold War language, but it should not be used as a lazy replacement for every undemocratic government.

Careful Version

Authoritarianism is the broader warning about concentrated power and weak accountability. Totalitarianism is the stronger claim: the state or ruling movement tries to reach deeply into society, thought, culture, media, association, and private life. Use the stronger word only when the source and evidence support it.
