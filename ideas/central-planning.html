Central Planning

Plain definition: Central planning means major production, investment, distribution, targets, and resource decisions are directed through state planning institutions instead of mainly through private market exchange.

Central planning is not the same thing as every public service. It is a stronger economic structure, especially when plans become binding commands across major sectors.

Why It Matters Here

Central planning matters because Soviet-style planning became one of the clearest practical differences between market capitalism and twentieth-century communist-party states. It also gives Hayek and Mises a real target when they argue about prices, knowledge, and calculation.

Careful Version

Planning has degrees. A household plans, a firm plans, a city plans, and a wartime state plans. Central planning means much more: an administrative system for directing major economic choices. Ask who writes the plan, what it controls, how errors are corrected, and whether people can contest it.
