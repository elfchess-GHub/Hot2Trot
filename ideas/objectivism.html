Objectivism

Plain definition: Objectivism is Ayn Rand's philosophy of reality, reason, self-interest, individual rights, and laissez-faire capitalism.

On this road, Objectivism matters because it gives capitalism a moral defense: productive achievement, individual rights, and voluntary exchange are treated as virtues, not just economic mechanisms.

Why It Matters Here

Objectivism matters because Rand turned market capitalism into a dramatic moral story. That story has influenced U.S. libertarian and conservative culture far beyond academic economics, especially through novels and public argument.

Careful Version

Do not treat Objectivism as the same thing as all capitalism, all libertarianism, or all conservatism. Rand has a specific moral philosophy: reason, egoism, individual rights, and laissez-faire capitalism. It is influential, contested, and not the only pro-market argument.
