Alienation

Plain definition: Alienation is a Marxist idea about people becoming separated from their work, the product they make, other people, and their own human powers under certain labor relations.

This is not just feeling bored at work. It is a claim about control: who owns the workplace, who directs the labor, who owns the product, and whether the worker recognizes themselves in what they make.

Why It Matters Here

Alienation matters because it turns the labor road from wages-only math into a human question. Marx is not only asking whether workers are paid enough. He is asking whether capitalist labor can make people strangers to their own time, creativity, bodies, and social life.

Careful Version

Do not use alienation as a generic word for sadness. In this study hall, alienation is a specific critique of labor relations. It connects to wage labor, private ownership, production for profit, and the gap between human capacity and controlled work.
