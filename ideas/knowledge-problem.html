Knowledge Problem

Plain definition: The knowledge problem is the argument that useful economic knowledge is scattered across people, places, moments, skills, needs, and local conditions.

In the Hayek path, this is a serious challenge to central planning: no single office can easily know everything people know locally.

Why It Matters Here

The knowledge problem matters because it makes the market debate more intelligent. The question is not only selfishness versus fairness. It is also how a society gathers information, changes direction, and coordinates millions of decisions.

Careful Version

The knowledge problem is not a magic proof that all planning fails. Families plan. Firms plan. Governments plan. The harder question is scale, feedback, coercion, democracy, and whether the plan can learn from local reality.
