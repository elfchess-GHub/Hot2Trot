Natural Liberty

Plain definition: Natural liberty is Adam Smith's language for a system where people are generally free to use their labor, property, exchange, and enterprise under law instead of under heavy mercantile restriction.

This is one of the cleanest pro-market ideas on the capitalism road, but it has to be tested against slavery, empire, monopoly, land seizure, and unequal bargaining power.

Why It Matters Here

Natural liberty matters because it gives capitalism its best self-description: people are not supposed to need permission from guilds, kings, monopolies, or planners to work, trade, and improve their condition. The balance question is whether that liberty is real for everyone or mostly protected for owners.

Careful Version

Smith is not Ayn Rand, and natural liberty is not a license for every corporation to do anything. Smith also discusses justice, public works, education, monopoly, and the duties of government. Keep the market argument strong, but do not turn it into a cartoon.
