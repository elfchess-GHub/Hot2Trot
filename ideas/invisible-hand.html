Invisible Hand

Plain definition: The invisible hand is Smith's image for unintended coordination: private gain can sometimes lead people toward broader social benefit without that being their original purpose.

In The Wealth of Nations, the famous passage is about capital, domestic industry, private interest, and unintended public effect. It is not a magic proof that every market outcome is good.

Why This Idea Matters

The invisible hand helps explain the strongest market argument: people do not need to love the public good for exchange and investment to sometimes serve it. The hard question is when that works, when it fails, and who gets harmed outside the transaction.

Careful Distinction

Use "sometimes." Smith's passage does not say greed always benefits everyone. It says unintended public benefit can happen under particular conditions. Hot2Trotski should teach the argument without turning it into market religion.
