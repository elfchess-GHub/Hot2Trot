Market

Plain definition: A market is a system where buyers and sellers exchange goods, services, labor, or assets, often through prices shaped by supply and demand.

Markets are not automatically capitalism by themselves. Markets can exist inside many systems. Capitalism is a broader structure built around private capital, wage labor, investment, profit, and property rules.

Why It Matters Here

Market matters because people often confuse “markets exist” with “capitalism is fully justified” or “public spending means socialism.” The real study question is what the market coordinates, who owns the productive property, who has bargaining power, and what public rules shape exchange.

Careful Version

Markets can coordinate information and opportunity. They can also hide coercion, monopoly, exclusion, pollution, debt pressure, and unequal starting points. A truth-first page has to study both the coordination power and the ledger of harm.
