Antitrust

Plain definition: Antitrust is law and policy aimed at protecting competition by limiting monopoly power, restraints of trade, price-fixing, and anti-competitive conduct.

On this road, antitrust is a democratic/legal counterweight to private market power. It is where capitalism admits that competition may need public rules to stay real.

Why This Idea Matters

Antitrust matters because a market can stop acting like a fair contest when one firm controls the door, the defaults, the infrastructure, or the price. Rockefeller and Gates belong here because Standard Oil and Microsoft both became examples of private power large enough to bring law back into the market story.

Where It Gets Tricky

Antitrust is not anti-business by default. It is about the difference between winning by better work and maintaining power by suppressing competition. The hard cases ask whether success came from merit, exclusion, control over access, predatory behavior, or political influence.
