Inflation

Plain definition: Inflation is a broad rise in the overall price level of goods and services, which reduces what money can buy.

Inflation belongs on the public-spending and monetarism roads because every promise about wages, prices, taxes, budgets, and money has to face purchasing power.

Why It Matters Here

Inflation matters because it can quietly change who gains and who loses. Wages, rent, savings, debt, public benefits, business costs, interest rates, and government budgets all feel price changes differently.

Careful Version

Do not reduce inflation to one cause without evidence. Money supply, demand, supply shocks, energy prices, wages, market power, war, expectations, and policy choices can all enter the argument. Friedman is important, but the page should not pretend every inflation debate is already settled.
