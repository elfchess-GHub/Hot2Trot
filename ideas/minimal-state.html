Minimal State

Plain definition: A minimal state is a government limited mainly to protecting people against force, theft, fraud, contract violation, and rights violations.

In the Nozick path, this is the libertarian boundary line: government may protect rights, but should not become a broad redistributive welfare state.

Why It Matters Here

Minimal state matters because it gives the anti-welfare-state side a serious argument. The claim is not simply “government bad.” The claim is that people have rights that limit what government may do, even when a majority wants a larger public program.

Careful Version

A minimal state is still a state. It still uses courts, police, law, taxation for its limited functions, and force against rights violations. So the honest question is not state or no state. It is how much state, for what purposes, under what rights theory.
