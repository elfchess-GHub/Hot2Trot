Settler Capitalism

Plain definition: Settler capitalism is a working label for capitalist expansion where settlers, private land claims, government policy, military force, and markets grow through Indigenous dispossession.

It joins two questions: who controls land, and how that land becomes property, settlement, production, credit, and profit.

Why It Matters Here

Andrew Jackson and Indian removal cannot be taught as only personality or frontier drama. Land became a political and economic prize, and the state cleared paths that private settlement, plantation expansion, speculation, railroads, and accumulation could use.

The careful claim is not that every settler was the same or every farm was the whole system. The careful claim is that U.S. capitalist development often worked through land made available by state-backed dispossession.

Mechanisms

State power: laws, armies, treaties under pressure, courts, and removal policy shape land access.
Private accumulation: land becomes farms, plantations, town lots, rail corridors, mines, mortgages, and speculation.
Political story: democracy for settlers can coexist with denied sovereignty for Native nations.
