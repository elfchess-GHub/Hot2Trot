Competition

Plain definition: Competition means buyers, sellers, firms, workers, or investors trying to win scarce opportunities, customers, resources, wages, profit, or market position.

On the capitalism road, competition is supposed to discipline prices, reward better products, punish waste, and keep monopoly power from hardening.

Why It Matters Here

Competition matters because many pro-market arguments depend on it. If competition is real, customers may get lower prices, better options, and more innovation. If competition is weak, markets can become monopoly, cartel, lock-in, political influence, or extraction.

Careful Version

Competition is not automatically fair. People do not enter markets with equal money, equal rights, equal information, equal safety, or equal bargaining power. The honest test is whether competition is open, lawful, informed, and strong enough to limit abuse.
