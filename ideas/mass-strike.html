Mass Strike

Plain definition: A mass strike is broad worker action that links economic pressure, political demands, organization, and public power across many workplaces or communities.

In the Luxemburg path, mass action is how ordinary people become political actors rather than spectators.

Why It Matters Here

Mass strike matters because it makes democracy active. People organize, stop work, assemble, and force public questions into view. Luxemburg treated the mass strike as a living historical process, not just a command issued by leaders.

Careful Version

A mass strike can be powerful, but it is not magic. It depends on organization, timing, repression, public support, economic pressure, and political clarity. The page should not romanticize action or dismiss it as chaos.
