Entrepreneurship

Plain definition: Entrepreneurship means organizing a new venture or economic activity under uncertainty, usually with risk, initiative, and the hope of creating value.

This room gives entrepreneurship real credit without turning it into worship. Initiative, invention, and coordination matter; so do ownership, labor, public support, monopoly, and who carries the downside.

Why It Matters Here

Capitalism often tells its best story through entrepreneurs: someone sees a possibility, takes a risk, organizes people and money, and builds something useful. The balanced study question is not whether entrepreneurship exists. It is who pays for risk, who captures reward, and when enterprise turns into extraction or monopoly.

Careful Distinction

Entrepreneurship is not identical to capitalism, and it is not identical to billionaires. People can organize new activity in private firms, cooperatives, public-private projects, social enterprises, and community systems. The label needs the ownership and power structure beside it.
