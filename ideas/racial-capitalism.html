Racial Capitalism

Plain definition: Racial capitalism is a way to study how capitalism and race-making can grow together: labor systems, property law, slavery, dispossession, migration control, credit, policing, housing, and profit do not always sit in separate boxes.

It does not mean every market exchange is only about race. It means race can be built into who gets land, wages, safety, credit, citizenship, and legal protection.

Why It Matters Here

Hot2Trotski needs this room because a fair capitalism road cannot skip slavery, Native dispossession, colonial extraction, Jim Crow, redlining, or unequal labor markets. Those are not side notes beside economic history. They shaped property, wealth, law, labor, and political power.

Core Moves

Labor: enslaved, coerced, excluded, migrant, and low-wage labor can be organized through racial categories.
Property: land, housing, inheritance, and credit rules can decide who builds wealth and who is blocked from it.
Law: race can be enforced through statutes, courts, policing, borders, and local practice.
Markets: shipping, insurance, cotton, sugar, railroads, housing, and finance can profit from unequal racial systems.

What To Be Careful About

Keep the claim historical and specific. Do not use “capitalism did it” as a shortcut that hides state violence, empire, local law, religion, war, or individual decision-making. Also do not excuse capitalist profit from slavery, segregation, or dispossession by pretending those were outside the economic system.

Mini-Check

Question: Why does plantation slavery belong in a capitalism study guide?

Answer: Because enslaved labor, land, cotton, sugar, shipping, insurance, credit, and racial law were tied to markets and wealth accumulation.
