State Power

Plain definition: State power is the organized capacity to make law, collect resources, administer institutions, command force, punish, govern territory, and define public order.

This idea belongs in every road. Capitalist states protect property and markets. Socialist revolutions try to seize or transform the state. Democracies try to limit state power through rights, elections, courts, and public accountability.

Why It Matters Here

State power is where political promises become institutions. A movement can promise liberation, order, markets, equality, security, or democracy; the state reveals what it can actually enforce, fund, permit, punish, and silence.

Key Distinction

State action is not automatically socialism. A state can serve landlords, slaveholders, corporations, workers, welfare institutions, parties, armies, or democratic publics. The question is: who controls the state, what does it enforce, and who benefits?

What To Be Careful About

Do not treat state power as bad only when the left uses it. Police, courts, borders, contracts, land titles, subsidies, war, removal policy, public spending, and repression can all be state power. The truth-first rule is to follow the power wherever it is working.

Mini-Check

Why does state power belong on both capitalism and socialism roads?

Because both private property systems and revolutionary governments depend on law, administration, coercion, and public institutions.
