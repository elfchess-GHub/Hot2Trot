Party-State

Plain definition: A party-state is a political system where the ruling party and state institutions are closely fused, so party authority shapes government, administration, appointments, law, security, planning, and public political life.

In the Stalin path, party-state is a specific structure. It is not the same thing as socialism, public spending, unions, or welfare policy.

Why It Matters Here

Party-state matters because it names how political power can harden after revolution. If one party controls the state, the question becomes whether dissent, elections, courts, unions, press, and ordinary people have any real independence left.

Careful Version

Do not use party-state as a smear word for every political party. The issue is institutional fusion: party authority and state authority become difficult to separate. That can happen under different ideologies, though communist-party states are central examples in this road.
