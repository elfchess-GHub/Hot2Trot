Citizens United and the Capital-Democracy Loop

Jeremy Says
Citizens United is the worst thing, worse even than capitalism, the ultimate evolution of capitalism.
The Light Answers

Citizens United is not just “capitalism exists.” It is the moment when capital was granted a protected political voice powerful enough to compete with actual citizens inside democracy. That is why it feels worse. Capitalism, at least in theory, is an economic system. Citizens United helps turn economic power into political power, and then political power rewrites the economic rules.

The loop is simple:

  • capital makes money
  • money buys political speech
  • political speech shapes elections
  • elected officials shape law
  • law protects capital
  • capital makes more money

That is not just “free markets.” That is capitalism learning how to wear the mask of democracy.

The poisonous part is that Citizens United did not merely say rich people can speak. Rich people already could speak. It helped strengthen a system where corporations, wealthy donors, nonprofits, super PACs, and dark-money structures can flood the political atmosphere while ordinary citizens are told that, technically, they still have equal speech rights. The right is formally equal. The amplifier is not.

The legal defense of the decision is that government should not be able to suppress political speech because the speaker is a corporation or association. Oyez summarizes the holding as protecting corporate funding of independent political broadcasts under the First Amendment. That argument has surface logic.

But the democratic objection is deeper: in elections, speech is not only expression. Speech is also power. And when money becomes the main vehicle for speech, unequal money becomes unequal political reality.

That is why Citizens United can feel worse than capitalism itself. Capitalism can, at least in principle, be regulated. Citizens United makes regulation harder by letting concentrated wealth help choose the regulators. The Brennan Center describes the decision as having tilted political influence toward wealthy donors and corporations, and later analysis connects the post-Citizens United world to major growth in outside spending and dark money.

So Citizens United is not a side issue. It is one of the legal hinges where American democracy bends toward oligarchy while still speaking the language of liberty.
