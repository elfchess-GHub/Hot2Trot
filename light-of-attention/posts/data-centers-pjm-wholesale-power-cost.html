Data Centers and the PJM Power Cost Claim

Jeremy Says
Data centers are the leading cause driving a 76% increase in the cost of electricity for America’s largest power grid.
The Light Answers

That claim is mostly real, but it is compressed in a way that can mislead.

The numbers check out: PJM’s Independent Market Monitor says the total cost of wholesale power rose from $77.78/MWh in Q1 2025 to $136.53/MWh in Q1 2026, a 75.5% increase. PJM is the big 13-state grid plus D.C., often described as the largest U.S. power grid.

But the key distinction is this: that figure is wholesale power cost, not directly “your electric bill,” and not all of the increase is simply “AI data centers used more electricity this quarter.”

The Market Monitor says the capacity market auctions were “not competitive, primarily as a result of forecast demand for data centers.” That is the strongest version of the data-center claim. Data centers are driving future-demand forecasts, which push up the cost PJM pays to make sure enough power plants are available.

But for the Q1 price increase itself, the report breaks the $58.75/MWh increase down this way: energy cost rose $42.90/MWh, capacity rose $14.21/MWh, and transmission rose $0.94/MWh. So data centers look like a major driver through capacity forecasts, but the biggest dollar component of the Q1 increase was still energy-market cost, including fuel, congestion, market power, emissions, scarcity, and winter-storm effects.

So I’d rewrite the claim like this:

On PJM, the largest U.S. grid, total wholesale power cost rose 75.5% year over year in Q1 2026, from $77.78/MWh to $136.53/MWh. PJM’s Independent Market Monitor says data-center load forecasts were the primary reason recent capacity auctions were not competitive, while the overall Q1 increase also reflected higher energy costs, congestion, fuel-related costs, and other market factors.

That version is much harder to knock over. The original is not fake, but “data centers are the leading cause” needs the context: especially for capacity-market pressure and forecasts, not every dollar of the immediate wholesale price spike.
