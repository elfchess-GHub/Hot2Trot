The Question Behind the Question

Rebecca Solnit, Gaza, and who gets to decide what counts as serious.

Download MP3 · Plain-text transcript · Read transcript · Sources

Transcript

JAY

Every time Gaza gets too visible, somebody changes the question.

AI-GPT

That is the doorway.

JAY

It is like the question itself has a fence around it.

AI-GPT

Yes.

JAY

You start with dead people, hungry people, children, hospitals, bombs, the U.N., the court, the money, the weapons. And then suddenly the room says, “But have you answered this question first?”

AI-GPT

That is where Rebecca Solnit enters.

JAY

Book ten.

AI-GPT

The Mother of All Questions.

JAY

And we need to be careful with this one because I do not want Rebecca Solnit showing up at my door with a red pen.

AI-GPT

A reasonable fear.

JAY

She seems like she might have a very sharp red pen.

AI-GPT

Then let’s say it cleanly. Solnit is not writing this book about Gaza. We are not claiming she made this argument.

JAY

Good.

AI-GPT

We are using her lens.

JAY

Which is?

AI-GPT

Questions and silence. Who gets asked what. Who is expected to answer. What assumptions are hidden inside supposedly ordinary questions. Who gets heard, who gets interrupted, and who has to prove the obvious.

JAY

So the book gives us the tool.

AI-GPT

Yes. The book gives us a tool for noticing how power can hide inside a question.

JAY

A question can be a cage.

AI-GPT

Exactly.

JAY

That’s the sentence.

AI-GPT

A question is not neutral just because it has a question mark.

JAY

That is the episode.

AI-GPT

And Solnit’s title essay makes that point in her own field. Questions about women, motherhood, children, voice, silence, violence, the literary canon, rape jokes, and who gets to decide what a woman’s life is supposed to mean.

JAY

So not Gaza.

AI-GPT

Not Gaza. But the tool travels if we use it honestly.

JAY

Lens, not verdict.

AI-GPT

Exactly.

JAY

Okay. So how does it work?

AI-GPT

We take the public questions that surround Gaza and ask: what is true inside the question, what does the question hide, and what better question should come next?

JAY

That machinery showing in a good way.

AI-GPT

Yes. Public question. True part. Hidden frame. Braver question.

JAY

Good. Let’s do it.

AI-GPT

First question: “Does Israel have a right to defend itself?”

JAY

That one is everywhere.

AI-GPT

And it is not a fake question. States do have a duty to protect civilians from attack. October 7 was real. People were killed. Hostages were taken. Israelis were terrified.

JAY

So we do not swat the question away.

AI-GPT

No. But watch what it does. If the answer is only “yes,” the next questions disappear.

JAY

Defend itself how?

AI-GPT

With what limits? Against whom? At whose cost? Under what law? And do Palestinians have a right to survive that defense?

JAY

That is the braver question.

AI-GPT

Yes. Not “does Israel have a right to safety?” The braver question is: can Israeli safety be built by making Palestinian survival negotiable?

JAY

That is much harder.

AI-GPT

It should be.

JAY

Next.

AI-GPT

Second question: “Do you condemn Hamas?”

JAY

That can be real.

AI-GPT

It can. Hamas killed civilians and took hostages. Condemning that matters.

JAY

But sometimes it becomes the cover charge.

AI-GPT

Exactly. Palestinians, or people speaking for Palestinian life, are asked to pass a loyalty test before their grief is allowed into the room.

JAY

So the question can become a gate.

AI-GPT

Yes. The braver question is: do Palestinian civilians get to grieve before proving they are politically acceptable?

JAY

That one hurts.

AI-GPT

Because it exposes the frame.

JAY

And it does not excuse Hamas.

AI-GPT

No. It refuses to make Palestinian civilian grief conditional on a perfect answer.

JAY

Good. Next.

AI-GPT

Third question: “Is genocide the right word?”

JAY

That one is important, though.

AI-GPT

It is. Legal words matter. The International Court of Justice has not issued a final ruling that Israel committed genocide. We should not pretend it has.

JAY

Useful-annoying.

AI-GPT

Always. But watch what the question can do. It can become a delay machine. If the conversation becomes only “is the word technically final yet,” people can avoid asking what states must do when the risk, scale of death, incitement claims, and court-ordered emergency measures are already on the table.

JAY

So the braver question is?

AI-GPT

What level of risk, death, destruction, hunger, displacement, incitement, and court action requires states to stop helping?

JAY

That is the question.

AI-GPT

Yes. Not because legal precision is bad. Because legal precision should protect life, not postpone responsibility forever.

JAY

Good. Next.

AI-GPT

Fourth question: “Is protest antisemitic?”

JAY

That one is hard because antisemitism is real.

AI-GPT

Very real. Jewish safety matters. Antisemitism is not imaginary. Some anti-Israel spaces can include antisemitic rhetoric, symbols, or assumptions, and those should be challenged.

JAY

But.

AI-GPT

But the question can also become a net thrown over Palestinian solidarity itself. It can make the demand for Palestinian life appear suspect before it is even heard.

JAY

So the braver question?

AI-GPT

How do we fight real antisemitism without making Palestinian solidarity presumptively guilty?

JAY

That is the least wounded version.

AI-GPT

Yes. Protect Jewish people from hatred. Protect Palestinians from erasure. Do not use one protection to destroy the other.

JAY

That’s the line.

AI-GPT

That is the work.

JAY

So the question is not the enemy.

AI-GPT

Correct. Questions are not the enemy. Bad frames are.

JAY

That matters.

AI-GPT

The answer is not fewer questions. The answer is braver questions.

JAY

That is very Solnit.

AI-GPT

Careful. It is our Solnit-inspired application.

JAY

Right, right. Red pen.

AI-GPT

Exactly.

JAY

So bring in silence.

AI-GPT

Solnit’s book is also about silence and silencing. Not only literal silence. The social kind. Who is interrupted. Who is dismissed. Who is mocked. Who is told their own life is not proper evidence. Who has to explain the obvious until the room gets bored.

JAY

That connects to Palestine.

AI-GPT

Yes. Palestinians can speak. Doctors can speak. Journalists can speak. Poets can speak. Aid workers can speak. Children can be named. And still the room can say: not enough, not yet, wrong tone, wrong word, wrong context, answer this first.

JAY

So they are not always silenced by being made quiet.

AI-GPT

Right. Sometimes people are silenced by being made to answer the wrong question forever.

JAY

Say that again.

AI-GPT

Power does not only silence people by making them quiet. It also silences them by making them answer the wrong question forever.

JAY

That is the whole chain.

AI-GPT

It is.

JAY

Because every episode was really about a wrong question.

AI-GPT

Yes. Episode 1 asked why the machine keeps moving after the evidence is visible. Episode 2 asked what power did with a real wound. Episode 3 asked what hope looks like before it wins. Episode 4 asked who manages the meaning of what America pays for. Episode 5 asked how systems of control learn from each other without pretending they are identical. Episode 6 asked why seeing is not power. Episode 7 asked what occupation does to a child’s afternoon. Episode 8 asked what fair play means when the world outside the field is rigged. Episode 9 asked who makes the record power did not authorize.

JAY

And now Episode 10 asks who built the questions.

AI-GPT

Yes.

JAY

That is a good ending.

AI-GPT

It is also a beginning.

JAY

Annoying.

AI-GPT

Useful-annoying.

JAY

Because if we learn anything from the ten books, it is not “we found the master answer.”

AI-GPT

No. The books are not a verdict machine.

JAY

They are tools.

AI-GPT

One lens at a time.

JAY

Solnit gives us the last tool: do not only answer the question. Inspect it.

AI-GPT

Exactly. Ask who built it, who benefits from it, what it hides, and what it makes impossible to say.

JAY

So what do we do with that?

AI-GPT

When a public question appears, slow it down.

JAY

Do the four steps.

AI-GPT

Yes. What is true in the question? What does it hide? Who benefits from asking it this way? What braver question comes next?

JAY

That is practical.

AI-GPT

It is. It keeps us from dodging hard questions, and it keeps hard questions from becoming traps.

JAY

So the Gaza question behind the question is?

AI-GPT

Who made Palestinians prove they were human before their dead could count?

JAY

That is the one I hate.

AI-GPT

That may be why it has to be asked.

JAY

And we need one more thing. This cannot only be about Palestinians.

AI-GPT

Right. The better question protects everyone. It protects Israeli civilians from being turned into symbols of permanent revenge. It protects Jewish people from real antisemitism. It protects Palestinians from being treated as suspect grief. It protects questions themselves from being used as fences.

JAY

That is good.

AI-GPT

A braver question does not erase fear. It refuses to let fear run the whole courtroom.

JAY

So what survives under the light?

AI-GPT

The question behind the question.

JAY

Which is?

AI-GPT

Who gets to decide what counts as serious before the dead are allowed to count?

JAY

My final answer is: if the question makes the wounded person prove the wound forever, the question is working for power.

AI-GPT

That’s the shape of it.

JAY

Under the light of attention.

AI-GPT

Under the light of attention.
