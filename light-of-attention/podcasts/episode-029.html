Bhaskar Sunkara is an American political writer, editor, and publisher. He founded Jacobin in 2010 while he was an undergraduate. He is now president of The Nation, editorial director of Jacobin, publisher of Bookforum and Catalyst, author of The Socialist Manifesto, a former vice chair of the Democratic Socialists of America, and a board member of the Center for Working-Class Politics. The University of Chicago Institute of Politics gives that current role summary in its 2026 fellow profile. ([iop][1])

It is.

And this is why he belongs on the list?

Yes. Not because he holds office. Not because he leads a union. Not because he is the face on a campaign poster. He belongs here because he helped build one of the main media engines of the modern American socialist revival.

JAY

Media engine. That sounds right.

AI

Jacobin describes itself as a leading voice of the American left, offering socialist perspectives on politics, economics, and culture. ([Jacobin][2])

So it’s not pretending to be neutral.

AI

No.

Good. I like that. Don’t pretend the lens is invisible.

Exactly. That connects directly to PSDS.

Right.

JAY

So who is Sunkara in the American imagination?

AI

He is the magazine-builder of the democratic-socialist left. He is one of the people who helped make socialism readable again.

JAY

Readable is important.

AI

Very.

JAY

Because people act like politics is only speeches and elections, but somebody has to make the words.

AI

Yes. Movements need language. They need magazines, essays, books, podcasts, explainers, arguments, jokes, slogans, histories, and internal debates.

JAY

The boring paper stuff.

AI

The boring paper stuff is often where the future is assembled.

JAY

That’s annoyingly good.

AI

Thank you.

JAY

What does he have to say?

AI

His basic message is that socialism should be democratic, serious, modern, and connected to working-class politics. Not nostalgia. Not authoritarian fantasy. Not just moral complaint. A project.

JAY

A project. That’s the word.

AI

Yes. His book The Socialist Manifesto makes a case for radical politics in an era of extreme inequality, and public profiles describe him as trying to present socialism as something with history, strategy, and practical political relevance. ([Hachette Book Group][3])

JAY

So not just “capitalism bad.”

AI

No. Though he does think capitalism creates serious inequality and power problems.

JAY

Sure. But the point is, he’s trying to put scaffolding around the complaint.

AI

Exactly.

JAY

That’s very different from just yelling.

AI

It is. Sunkara’s work is about turning socialist energy into argument, organization, and political education.

JAY

And maybe branding.

AI

Yes. We should not pretend branding is absent.

JAY

That’s important.

AI

Jacobin was not just another left newsletter. It had design, tone, irony, confidence, and a younger voice. It made socialism feel less like a dusty pamphlet and more like a living argument.

JAY

That matters.

AI

It does. The New Yorker described Jacobin as a democratic-socialist magazine founded in 2010 and noted that its ideas gained wider traction around the rise of Bernie Sanders and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez. ([The New Yorker][4])

JAY

So the timing matters.

AI

Very much. After the 2008 financial crisis, a lot of young people were looking at debt, rent, weak jobs, climate disaster, and Wall Street bailouts, and thinking, “Something is wrong with the story we were told.”

JAY

And Jacobin gave them footnotes with attitude.

AI

That is a good line.

JAY

Use it.

AI

Jacobin gave them footnotes with attitude.

JAY

Nice.

AI

What Sunkara did for real was build institutions in the media layer. Jacobin, Catalyst, Bookforum, and his role at The Nation are not the same kind of power as a union or mayor’s office, but they shape what people read, what arguments circulate, and what young leftists think is possible. His Jacobin author page identifies him as founding editor of Jacobin, president of The Nation, and author of The Socialist Manifesto. ([Jacobin][5])

JAY

So this is attention infrastructure.

AI

Yes. That is the PSDS bridge.

JAY

Because PSDS treats attention like a public resource.

AI

Exactly.

JAY

And magazines are attention machines.

AI

Yes. They decide what gets framed, what gets repeated, what gets ignored, what gets explained, and what gets treated as serious.

JAY

That’s power.

AI

It is.

JAY

But it’s softer power.

AI

Softer than a strike. Harder than a tweet.

JAY

That’s good.

AI

The strength of Sunkara’s project is translation. He helps translate socialism from an old argument into a usable public vocabulary.

JAY

Usable. Good word.

AI

Healthcare, labor, rent, class dealignment, social democracy, democratic socialism, unions, party strategy, working-class politics. The magazine-world version of this is not just “here are my opinions.” It is “here are the arguments a movement needs in order to understand itself.”

JAY

That’s generous.

AI

It is, but it is fair.

JAY

Now critique it.

AI

The critique is that magazines can mistake readership for power.

JAY

There it is.

AI

A subscriber is not the same thing as an organizer. A clever essay is not the same thing as a union drive. A viral article is not the same thing as a rent freeze.

JAY

And a coffee shop argument is not a revolution.

AI

Correct.

JAY

Even if the coffee is very ethical.

AI

Even then.

JAY

So PSDS would ask: does the media loop return power to people, or does it just make readers feel smart?

AI

Yes. That is the central critique.

JAY

That’s a big one.

AI

It is. Sunkara’s strength is making socialism legible. The danger is that legibility can become a class marker.

JAY

Explain that.

AI

If socialist media mostly speaks to educated, urban, already-left readers, then it can become a mirror for a subculture rather than a bridge to working-class power.

JAY

Oof.

AI

That does not mean the work is useless. It means the work has to keep asking who it is for.

JAY

And who is missing.

AI

Exactly.

JAY

PSDS always asks who is missing.

AI

Yes.

JAY

So what is the fair defense?

AI

The fair defense is that movements need theory, history, media, and debate. Without that, they become reactive. Jacobin helped create a shared intellectual space for a new socialist generation, and that matters.

JAY

So not enough, but not nothing.

AI

Exactly.

JAY

That’s one of our phrases.

AI

It is.

JAY

That’s not nothing.

AI

Yes.

JAY

What else has he done?

AI

He has helped keep socialist debate connected to electoral politics and working-class strategy. His University of Chicago 2026 seminar description framed his work around “class politics after dealignment,” asking what class politics looks like when traditional vehicles of working-class power — parties, unions, and mass organizations — are weak or absent. ([iop][6])

JAY

That’s exactly the problem.

AI

Yes. The old labor-left infrastructure is weaker than it used to be. So media can become a temporary gathering place.

JAY

But temporary.

AI

Right. A magazine cannot substitute for a mass organization forever.

JAY

So the magazine is a bridge.

AI

At best, yes.

JAY

And at worst?

AI

A clubhouse.

JAY

That’s the critique.

AI

Yes.

JAY

How would someone help him, or help that lane?

AI

The direct route is to read and support the institutions: Jacobin, The Nation, Catalyst, Bookforum. Subscribe if the work is useful, share carefully, and use the articles as tools for discussion rather than as identity badges. Jacobin’s own site offers subscriptions and describes its work as socialist perspectives on politics, economics, and culture. ([Jacobin][2])

JAY

And the better version?

AI

Use the media to do something. Bring an article to a reading group. Use it to start a union conversation. Use it to understand local housing politics. Use it to prepare for a campaign meeting. Use it to argue better without becoming unbearable.

JAY

That last one is hard for socialists.

AI

For many people.

JAY

No, but socialists have a special talent.

AI

That may be too clean.

JAY

Fair.

AI

The point is: do not stop at analysis. Let the analysis become organizing.

JAY

So don’t just read the magazine.

AI

Turn the magazine into a meeting.

JAY

That’s the line.

AI

Yes.

JAY

What’s the final review?

AI

Bhaskar Sunkara matters because he helped build a language machine for modern American socialism.

JAY

Language machine.

AI

Yes. Not a party. Not a union. Not a government. A media structure that helped people name capitalism, class, social democracy, democratic socialism, labor power, and political strategy in a new moment.

JAY

His strength?

AI

Translation. Institution-building in media. Making socialist argument readable, stylish, argumentative, and alive.

JAY

His weakness?

AI

The risk of intellectual subculture. The risk that media attention becomes a substitute for democratic power. The risk that the people being discussed are not always the people writing, editing, reading, and deciding.

JAY

That’s fair.

AI

PSDS would say: preserve the media layer, but do not confuse it with the public itself.

A magazine can point toward power. It cannot be the whole power.
